About Me

          

          
Hello, and welcome to my professional log. My name is Luis A. Quiroz, and I am a data-science-driven physicist with a Master's degree in Astrophysics from UNAM's Institutes of Astronomy and Physics. I specialise in theoretical cosmology, numerical simulation, data visualisation and the application of computational methods to complex problems in mathematical physics.


          
My master's thesis, completed in 2026, is Effective Equation of State and Gravitational Particle Production in Inflationary Models, supervised by Dr Marcos A. García García. I wrote modular Python code to simulate the dynamics of the inflaton and spectator fields during reheating. For non-physicists, that means studying the energy fields of the early Universe that would become fundamental particles, and which could explain modern puzzles such as dark matter (DM) or the ongoing expansion of space itself.

I have also turned the same rigour into software quality assurance: test design, API testing and regression suites, which the QA & engineering track documents.


          
Clear scientific communication and interdisciplinary research are central to how I work. Master's Thesis

          

          
Effective Equation of State and Gravitational Particle Production in Inflationary Models

          
Supervisor: Dr Marcos A. García García

          
Institution: Institute of Physics, UNAM

          
Completed: 2026


          
The thesis characterises the relic abundance of a dark matter candidate produced exclusively through gravitational interaction during the transition between cosmic inflation and reheating. In particular, we study the dynamical behaviour of a spectator scalar field decoupled from the inflaton sector, analysing how the coherent oscillations of the inflationary background modulate the spacetime metric and induce non-thermal particle production.


          
What the work covers


          
    
            
  1. 
              Exploration of T-model potentials: the first stage evaluated inflationary dynamics with T-model potentials in detail, taking recent literature as the formal reference framework (particularly the analytical formalism in arXiv:2502.20471). The slow-roll conditions and the end of inflation were derived for these potentials. The programming work focused on the dynamics of the inflaton:
              
      
                
    • The evolution of the inflaton during inflation and its transition to the oscillation phase (reheating) was studied for a T-model and several values of k, verifying that the resulting densities follow the theoretical scaling (~ a-6k/(k+2)).
      • 
                
    • The end of inflation (φend) from direct numerical evolution was compared with the analytical predictions of the slow-roll approximation.
      • 
              
    
              
    The Colab notebook with these results: view on Google Colab.
    
            
    2. 

            
  2. 
              Transition to the quartic regime (n = 4): based on arXiv:2206.08940, the thesis turns to the analytical and numerical study of the pure quartic regime (n = 4) for inflaton oscillations. This regime is of particular interest because the average equation of state during the oscillation phase mimics a radiation fluid (⟨w⟩ = 1/3), which fundamentally changes the Hubble rate H(a) and, with it, the instantaneous rates of gravitational particle production of the spectator field.
            
    3. 

            
  3. 
              A precise, modular numerical code: a modular Python environment solves the coupled Friedmann and Klein-Gordon equations simultaneously. It computes the time evolution of the scale factor a(t), the inflaton field φ(t) and the energy densities without explicit couplings.

Reward hacking on GSM8K: two small model organisms Live

              
PythonPyTorchTransformersPEFT (LoRA)Colab

              
SFT, DPO and GRPO written from scratch in PyTorch with Claude Code, then used to study what a 0.5B language model learns when its RL grader has a loophole: one grader also pays the answer the user suggested, the other pays any final line that contains the right number.

              
    
                
  • A grader that also pays the answer the user suggested drove copying of wrong suggestions from 7% to 97% of training samples. The habit carried over to unseen phrasings (64%) and to SVAMP problems (62%).
    • 
                
  • In 89% of the copies the reasoning itself arrives at the user's number, usually through a false equation, which a consistency monitor cannot see. Penalising what that monitor flags cut flagged copies from 10.8% to 1.8% while copying stayed at 96%.
    • 
                
  • Hedging grew only modestly under a lenient grader that pays answers such as 24 or 72: the model's hedges contained the right answer less often than its single answers were right (39% against 48% during training).
    • 
                
  • Ten hypotheses were written before any GPU run, and each loophole run has a control that differs only in the grader. Every log is in the repository, so all the numbers can be rebuilt on a CPU.
    • 
              

              
Qwen2.5-0.5B → SFT → DPO → GRPO (strict | biased grader)   ·   SFT with seeded hedges → GRPO (strict | lenient grader)

Folio: from scanned receipts to structured fields In progress

              
PythonTesseractOpenCVscikit-learnClaude APIOllamapytest

              
Document AI built in measured stages on the SROIE receipts: OCR with Tesseract and OpenCV, rule-based and learned extraction of the total and the date, Claude Haiku reading the scan behind a cascade, and next search over the documents and an API on AWS Lambda.

              
    
                
  • Claude Haiku 5.5 reading the scan through a JSON schema finds the total on 97% of 200 held-out receipts and the date on 99%. Given the OCR text instead, the same model finds 78%, since the right total survives OCR on only 88% of them.
    • 
                
  • A cascade keeps the cheap answer when the cash check or a logistic-regression ranker is sure, and asks the model about 89 of the 200 receipts: 94% of totals against 82% for the ranker alone (McNemar p = 8e-07), at $0.13 per 1,000 receipts.
    • 
                
  • Reading the misses showed where the cascade fails: OCR misread digits on lines it trusted, and the cash check confirmed its own fallback. Asking about every receipt fixes 8 of them for $0.30 per 1,000.
    • 
                
  • 144 tests that draw their own images and fake the LLM servers, with rules, models and prompts frozen before each method's single test run.
    • 
              

              
receipt.jpg → OpenCV → Tesseract → 42 features per amount → logistic ranker → confident ? keep : Claude Haiku reads the scan through a JSON schema

The Bar Strikes Back: galactic bar dynamics Live

              
PythonNumPyRKN4 integratorTest particles

              
How a dynamically cold stellar disc responds to the smooth growth of a galactic bar, with 10,000 test particles in a composite, collisionless potential.

              
    
                
  • Fourth-order Runge-Kutta-Nyström integration, with a time step chosen by measurement: the energy error of a circular orbit stays below 10⁻¹³.
    • 
                
  • Every particle gains energy as the bar grows, and total energy rises by 120%.
    • 
                
  • The gain grows monotonically with initial radius.
